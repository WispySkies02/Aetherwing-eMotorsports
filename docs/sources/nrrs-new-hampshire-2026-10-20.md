## An Unexpected Start

**LOUDON, N.H.** — After a frustrating stretch of races that threatened to derail her postseason ambitions, Hailey Bell arrived at New Hampshire Motor Speedway with modest expectations.

What followed in qualifying surprised nearly everyone.

Bell put the No. 32 Toyota on pole for the penultimate race of the NRRS Town Fair Cup Series season, outperforming a field that included championship leaders Will and Clutch.

The result was particularly unexpected given Bell's struggles on shorter tracks throughout the year.

Even more remarkably, Bell admitted she had not been entirely focused on the qualifying lap, having been distracted by the championship standings.

For a driver who had spent much of the season searching for competitive speed, the pole provided a rare moment of encouragement.

But qualifying speed and race pace would prove to be two very different things.

## Early Aggression Comes at a Cost

Bell's promising starting position quickly gave way to frustration once the race began.

Attempting to carry speed through New Hampshire's relatively flat corners, Bell entered the turns too aggressively, placing excessive demands on her tires.

The consequences became increasingly apparent as the run continued.

With tire performance deteriorating, the No. 32 became more difficult to handle, compromising Bell's ability to maintain competitive lap times.

Rather than being able to build upon her qualifying performance, Bell found herself losing ground as the race progressed.

Bell acknowledged that her driving approach had contributed to the problem.

"I dove it in the turns too much and that's what used them up quick," Bell explained, adding that handling and overall speed became problems as a result.

The admission reflected a frustrating reality: a race that had started with unexpected promise was being undermined by a mistake Bell believed she had made herself.

Despite finishing fourth in the opening stage and third in the second, she could not recover the pace needed to challenge for the victory.

Bell eventually crossed the finish line in third place, two laps behind the race winner.

## A Podium That Tells Only Part of the Story

On paper, New Hampshire marked an improvement over Bell's previous two outings.

At Talladega, she had finished fourth despite winning a stage and leading the most laps.

At Portland, she struggled throughout the road-course event and finished eighth without scoring stage points.

New Hampshire produced a third-place result, five stage points and a move up the championship standings.

Yet the result did not reflect a race in which Bell consistently matched the leaders.

Her surprise pole demonstrated that the speed was there over a single lap. The race itself demonstrated how quickly that advantage could disappear.

Clutch ultimately claimed the victory, his second of the season, while Will finished runner-up.

Bell's third-place finish placed her ahead of Matty, but the two-lap deficit illustrated the distance between her qualifying potential and sustained race performance.

For Bell, it was a result worth having, even if the race itself left considerable room for improvement.

## Third in the Championship — But Not Yet Secure

New Hampshire's most significant development for Bell came in the championship standings.

With Trent and Outlaw both absent, Bell's 39-point performance moved her from fourth to third in the Chase.

Regardless, Bell now controls her own path toward the third-place championship finish she has been targeting.

## One Race Left to Prove Something

The NRRS Town Fair Cup Series now heads to Homestead for its 25th and final race of the season.

For Bell, the championship title itself is out of reach, but the opportunity to finish third remains firmly in her hands.

Her New Hampshire performance represented both progress and another reminder of the challenges that have defined her season.

She qualified on pole.

She struggled with tire management.

She lost significant ground during the race.

And she still finished third.

The combination reflects a season in which Bell has repeatedly demonstrated flashes of competitiveness without consistently converting them into victories.

With her 2027 move to Starclutch Racing approaching, Homestead also represents the final opportunity to close her current NRRS campaign on a stronger note.

The New Hampshire podium may have improved her position in the standings.

But the work required to compete consistently at the front remains unfinished.

**One race remains. One final opportunity to turn promise into performance.**
